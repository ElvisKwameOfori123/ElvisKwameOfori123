# Hi, I'm Elvis Kwame Ofori

**PhD Researcher & Research Assistant | Biophysical, Agricultural, Land-Use & Policy Modelling | University of Galway (Ryan Institute)**

## About me

I am a PhD Researcher and Research Assistant in Plants & AgriBioscience, School of Biological & Chemical Sciences, Ryan Institute, University of Galway. My work sits at the intersection of agricultural and environmental economics, biophysical and land-use modelling, climate policy and quantitative decision support.

I develop **biophysical, economic, spatial, behavioural and computational modelling tools** to examine how climate, agricultural, land-use, food-system and bioenergy policies interact with production systems, resource constraints and economic decisions. My research connects national and global policy objectives with the farms, industries, landscapes and communities through which transitions take place. I am particularly interested in **where adjustment occurs, who bears the costs, which opportunities become feasible and how policy design shapes the outcome**.

My research spans **Ireland, the United States and wider international and global applications**, combining AFOLU and resource-system modelling with econometrics, microsimulation, optimisation, spatial analysis and scenario-based assessment. Across these contexts, I aim to make the assumptions, trade-offs and distributional consequences of alternative pathways visible rather than reducing complex transitions to a single national outcome.

At the University of Galway, my work sits within the wider **FORESIGHT** and **LandingZoNES** research environment and builds on the **GOBLIN** modelling framework. I am also interested in developing reusable, transparent modelling and decision-support systems that other researchers and policy analysts can test, adapt and extend.

## Strategic foresight and policy design

For me, strategic foresight is not the prediction of one inevitable future. It is a way to examine plausible pathways, challenge assumptions and stress-test decisions before technological, environmental or institutional commitments become difficult to reverse. My modelling work considers how national and global objectives translate into heterogeneous responses across production systems, farms and places, and under what spatial, biophysical, economic and behavioural conditions different interventions may succeed or encounter constraints.

These are distinct research projects rather than a single operationally integrated model. Together, they reflect a wider approach to **anticipatory, place-sensitive and distributionally informed policy analysis**. My broader interests also extend to international food, energy, land and resource-system interactions.

## Research themes

- **Climate, agriculture and land-use transitions:** AFOLU mitigation, livestock restructuring, land-use competition, afforestation, rewetting, bioenergy and agricultural productivity.
- **Biophysical and spatial modelling:** land capability, environmental constraints, livestock systems, spatial reconstruction and resource allocation.
- **Economic and behavioural responses:** producer decisions, adjustment costs, policy incidence, incentives, compensation and heterogeneous transition effects.
- **Food, energy and development:** food-system sustainability, energy transitions, carbon pricing and climate-development trade-offs across national and global contexts.
- **Strategic foresight:** alternative pathways, scenario stress-testing, uncertainty, feasibility and anticipatory policy design.

## Methods and tools

**Modelling:** biophysical and AFOLU accounting, synthetic farm populations, behavioural microsimulation, discrete choice, spatial allocation, optimisation, scenario analysis and stress-testing.

**Quantitative analysis:** panel econometrics, agricultural and environmental economics, distributional analysis, sensitivity testing and model validation.

**Programming and reproducibility:** Python, R, Stata, GIS and Excel-based models; version-controlled workflows, documented assumptions and reproducible analytical pipelines.

## Reproducible research

I use GitHub to document scientific assumptions, organise analytical pipelines and make modelling choices easier to inspect and reproduce. Where data-access conditions permit, my aim is to share model code, validation checks, scenario configurations, methodological documentation and worked examples. Access-restricted microdata remain protected.
